# Demonstration and next validation

## A two-minute screen recording

Introduce yourself as Ankit Prajapati, an IIT Hyderabad M.Tech Climate Change student with an IT background. Say FloodOps is an early prototype built in November 2026, not a deployed emergency service.

Show the labelled synthetic warning. Explain that forecasting and official alert distribution already exist; your proposed contribution is helping a local coordinator review and record preparation. Build the checklist, inspect a source note, assign roles, and demonstrate that review is required before progress can be saved. Record a task, export the brief and explain that no message has been sent.

If showing the optional local model, say it is an experiment and show the actual result, including a withheld/poor result where applicable. Do not edit a recording to imply successful AI output or a real institution’s approval. End with the next milestone: a supervised drill with one willing organisation and a comparison against its normal process.

## Proposed user review — not yet completed

Ask a school or community-organisation coordinator to review the prototype voluntarily. Do not describe them as a partner or beneficiary without their agreement. Ask:

1. What do you do after receiving a heavy-rain or flood warning?
2. Where do responsibilities or confirmations get lost?
3. Would a separate workspace help, or add work to an existing system?
4. Which tasks are inappropriate, missing or incorrectly assigned?
5. What evidence would justify using this during a drill?

Record consent, date, role, feedback and the resulting design changes. One interview is input, not proof of adoption or impact.

## Proposed evaluation

Use a tabletop exercise with the organisation’s approved procedures. Compare its existing process and FloodOps on time to a reviewed checklist, missed/misassigned tasks, acknowledgement completeness, stale/cancelled warning handling, accessibility and coordinator workload. Define success with participants before measurement. Separate software defects, model errors and workflow limitations. Do not claim lives saved, reduced losses or minutes saved without suitable evidence.
